01

Big idea first

David Bayer's four beats of a message that converts

People buy beliefs before they buy products.

what it is

Every message has four parts, in this order. Stacked together, they build trust.

  1. Big idea. The one belief that makes people curious.
  2. Story. Your real story, so they think "that's me."
  3. Framework. Three simple beliefs that make the big idea true.
  4. Proof. Real people it worked for.

how I used it

"Your dog doesn't have an obedience problem. Your dog has a job problem."

That is the big idea for Scentsible K9. The framework is three beliefs: behavior is information, fulfillment beats suppression, and the leash has two ends.

I wrote it once, in one document. My website, posts, emails and booth pitch all pull from it. I never invent a new message. I deliver the same one in different formats.

02

Your customer is the hero

StoryBrand, from Donald Miller's book Building a StoryBrand

Your customer is the hero. You are the guide.

what it is

Every good story works the same way. Your website should too.

  1. A hero wants something
  2. and has a problem.
  3. They meet a guide
  4. who gives them a plan
  5. and calls them to act.
  6. That helps them avoid failure
  7. and ends in success.

how I used it

"You love your dog. You're just exhausted."

That is the headline on scentsiblek9training.com. It is about the owner, not about us. It names their problem in the words they would use.

Most business websites make themselves the hero: our awards, our team, our years in business. Flip it. The customer is the star. You are the one who helps.

03

Stop competing. Make your own lane.

Blue Ocean Strategy, by W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne

Don't fight over the same customers with the same offer. Change the offer.

what it is

A red ocean is where everyone sells the same thing and fights on price. A blue ocean is a space nobody else is in yet.

To find yours, answer four questions about your offer:

  • Eliminate: what does everyone do that you can drop?
  • Reduce: what can you do less of?
  • Raise: what can you do far better?
  • Create: what can you offer that nobody does?

how I used it

Red ocean: every trainer in my valley sells control. Sit, stay, stop pulling, a quieter dog. They compete on price.

Blue ocean: we teach dogs to do more, not less. Dogs were bred to work, so we give them jobs: scent work, sports, tricks, even a dog acting academy.

"Most training teaches dogs to do less. We teach them to do more."

04

Pay yourself first

Profit First, by Mike Michalowicz

Take your profit first. Run the business on what is left.

what it is

Most owners pay everyone else, then hope something is left for them. Profit First flips the order.

When money comes in, a set percentage moves right away into separate accounts: profit, owner pay and taxes. The business runs on the rest. If the rest is not enough, that tells you something.

how I am using it

My business coach had me read this book this year. I am setting it up now, and step one was cleaning up my books with AI so the numbers are real.

One rule I learned the hard way: AI is not a calculator. Any math it does for my money runs in code, with the work shown, so I can check it.

My design rules

Mine. Learned by shipping a lot of pages.

Calm, clear, and checked on a real phone.

the rules

  • Light and airy first screen. No dark takeovers.
  • Four things on a card, max. More than that and nobody reads it.
  • Fill the page. No screen that is mostly blank.
  • One spacing scale. The same few gaps everywhere, so it all lines up.
  • Smooth, not bouncy. Soft fades only. No spins, pops or moving widgets.
  • Logo on every piece.
  • Check it at phone size before it ships.

how I use them

I don't open a design app. I saved these rules once, and my robots check every page against them before they show me.

This website was built that way. So were my dog training site, my class posters and my print pieces.

06

My marketing rules

Mine. Every one came from a real mistake.

Lead with the why. Never fake the proof.

the rules

  • Mission and heart first. Lead with why you do it, then what you sell.
  • Real reviews only, word for word. Never invent one, never reword one.
  • One message, every channel. See rule 01.
  • Short texts. If it scrolls, it is too long.
  • Every comment reply is different. Nobody likes a copy and paste.
  • Nothing goes out until I say send.

how I use them

My robots draft my posts, emails, texts and comment replies. They check them against these rules first. Then I read every one and say send.

AI can sound like you. It cannot care like you. That part stays mine.
